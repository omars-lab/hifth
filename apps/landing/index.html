Hifth

Revise the Qur'an on the page you memorised it from.

Open Hifth in your browser

Mac and iPhone apps — coming soon

What you can do with it

The 604-page mus'haf, line for line, so a verse sits where your memory puts it. Everything else is built around that page.

Mark your mistakes

Tap the word you slipped on and say what went wrong: the word itself, its harakat, or going on into another verse.

Look-alike verses, one tap apart

Verses that are easy to mix up are linked. Jump between them and see the words that differ.

Drop notes

Leave a note on a word, a verse or a whole passage, and find them all again on one page.

Review your progress

A month's calendar of the days you opened the mus'haf and the mistakes you marked on each, and a map of every ayah showing how long since you last opened it. It counts what you opened; whether you revised it is yours to say.

Share a verse

Send family or a friend a link that opens the exact verse or passage you were reading, on its own or with its look-alikes.

Coming next

  • Share with your teacher. Send your teacher where you slipped, only when you choose to, and see who can still open it.
  • Reflect on each verse. Commentary beside the verse you are on, so you can stop and think about what you recite.
  • Open Hifth from other apps. Links that open a verse from Shortcuts, a reminder or your notes, with the Mac and iPhone apps.

On your devices

Choose your device

In any browser on your computer, the mus'haf opens as two facing pages, the way the book lies open.

Open Hifth in your browser

On a phone, one page at a time, turned with your thumb. Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app, and keeps working offline.

Open Hifth on your phone

In the browser on an iPad, the page is held like the book, and a stylus marks a mistake straight on the word.
