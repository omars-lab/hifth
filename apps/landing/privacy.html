What Hifth keeps about you

This page covers the Hifth apps for iPad, iPhone and Mac, the web app at hifth.app/read, and this website.

What stays on your device

Hifth keeps your marks, notes, bookmarks and reading on your own device. The apps keep them in their own storage, and the web app keeps them in your browser. None of it is sent to us, and we have no copy of it.

When the web app moves from its old address to hifth.app, the old page hands your marks to the new one inside your own browser. They do not pass through us on the way.

Recitation

When you play a verse, the app fetches its recitation from verses.quran.com, the Quran.com library's sound files. So there is no pause, it also asks for a verse's sound as soon as you open that verse's tools or a menu that offers to play it, and for the next few verses while one plays. Set "The next verses in a recitation" to Off in the ⓘ panel to stop those early requests; then a verse is fetched only when it starts. That site sees each request, as any site sees a visit, and its own privacy terms cover it. Nothing else in the app fetches from another site.

The iPad, iPhone and Mac apps from the App Store leave the recitation out for now, so they fetch nothing from verses.quran.com while you read. The one thing they ask for is the small file in the next section.

A check for problems, each time the app opens

Each time it opens, the iPad, iPhone or Mac app asks hifth.app for one small file. The file names the oldest version of the app that still works well. If a version has a problem, it can also carry a short notice, or switch off writing with the Apple Pencil or links from other apps for a while.

The request carries nothing about you: no account, no device name, no notes or marks, and not even which version you have, because your app compares the versions itself. Like any visit to a website, hifth.app's host, Cloudflare, sees the request arrive; we keep no record of it. With no connection, the app carries on as before.

A problem report, only if you send one

"Report a problem" in the app shows you the whole report first. It holds the app's version, the kind of device, the screen size, the language, the time, the last few errors and the names of your last few steps, such as "turned to another page", never which page. The steps are kept only in the app's memory and are gone when you close it. It never holds your notes, marks, bookmarks, a list of the pages you read, or anything you typed. It opens in your own mail app, addressed to hello@hifth.app, and nothing is sent until you press send there. How to send one.

The iPad, iPhone and Mac apps also keep, on your device, the last five short reports Apple's system hands them after the app crashes or freezes. Each says when it happened, what kind it was and the app's version, and for a crash the places in the app's code it stopped; never what you were reading or anything you typed. They leave your device only inside a problem report you choose to send, where you see them first.

If you have chosen in your device's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may send us reports of the app crashing. Apple strips your personal details from them first, and you can turn this off in the same settings.

Accounts

Hifth has no accounts yet. If sign-in comes, this page will say exactly what an account stores, and how to delete it from inside the app, before it opens.

The "tell me when the apps are out" list

If you leave your email on the front page, we send one email with a button to confirm it. You are only on the list once you press it. We use the list for one thing: to write when the Mac and iPhone apps ship, and perhaps to invite you to try them early. We do not share it or sell it, and we do not add you to anything else.

For each address we keep the email, when you left it, when you confirmed it, the language you used, and the code in your leave link. An address nobody confirms is deleted after 7 days.

The list is stored in a Supabase database that only the person who runs Hifth can read. The website itself can only add, confirm or remove an address; it cannot read the list. The service that sends the emails will be named here before the list opens.

Every email we send has a link that takes you off the list at once. To leave before then, write to hello@hifth.app.

Spam check

The form uses Cloudflare Turnstile to tell people from bots. It runs in your browser when you use the form, and Cloudflare's own privacy terms cover it.

No tracking

The apps and this site have no analytics, no advertising and no tracking cookies. No outside company receives anything about how you use Hifth.
