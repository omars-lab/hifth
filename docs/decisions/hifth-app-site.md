# Where should the reading app live once hifth.app has a front page?

*Status: decided by the owner on 11 November 2026: the app moves to hifth.app/read now. Two
choices made with it are recorded here as well: the page says "Mac app — coming soon", and it
collects emails for one "it's out" message.*

## A few words, defined once

- **Front page**: what someone sees when they type hifth.app: one line on what Hifth is for, a
  button that opens the app, and a word about the Mac app.
- **Old address**: blog.bytesofpurpose.com/hifth/, where the reading app has lived so far.
- **Marks**: a reader's bookmarks, notes and look-alike jumps. A browser keeps them under the
  exact address they were made at, so a new address starts with none.

## What is being decided?

Whether the reading app stays at the old address with the new front page linking to it, or
moves under the new address at the same time.

## What does it change for a hafiz?

> **For a hafiz:** one short address to remember and to give a friend. The pages, marks and
> look-alike jumps are the same. A reader who used the old address is offered a way to carry
> their marks across, so moving does not cost them their bookmarks mid-revision.

## Why was this asked now?

The owner bought hifth.app on 10 November 2026 and asked for a front page that announces the Mac
app. Where its "open the app" button points had to be settled before the page could be built.

## What happens if nobody decides?

The page could not be built: its main button needs an address.

## What did the app do before?

The app lived only at the old address. There was no front page, no way to hear about the Mac
app, and no address of Hifth's own, which the sharing research had said a later sign-in or
share page would need ([sharing between readers](../design/sharing-between-readers.md), "Does
it need a separate site?").

## What were the options?

| option | pros | cons | what it commits us to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A**: the app stays at the old address; the front page links to it (recommended at the time) | nothing moves; readers keep their marks without doing anything | two addresses for now | moving the app later, when a sign-in or share page needs its own address |
| **B**: move the app to hifth.app/read now | one address from day one; a later sign-in has a home ready | marks saved at the old address stay there unless carried; a page-only job becomes moving the whole app | a way to carry marks across, and telling readers at the old address |
| **C**: put the front page at the old address and leave hifth.app unused | no new hosting | ignores the address just bought; the file Apple needs before a link can open the app cannot sit at the blog's root | nothing new, and nothing gained |

## What else was chosen with it?

- **What the page promises about the Mac app.** Offered: "Mac app — coming soon"
  (recommended), "Coming soon to the Mac App Store", or the web app alone. Chosen: **coming
  soon**, with no store name and no store badge. Selling on the App Store still waits on someone
  qualified reading the Qur'an data licences, and Apple's badge is meant to point at a live
  listing. The store wording is a roadmap row for once the app is listed.
- **Whether the page collects emails.** Offered: no list (recommended), or a notify-me form.
  Chosen: **the form**. That brings a privacy page, a way to leave the list, and a spam check,
  all built ([the site's open items](../design/hifth-app-site.md)).

## What would change the answer?

If carrying marks across turns out to lose them for real readers, the move could be paused by
leaving the "Hifth has moved" card switched off. The old address keeps working either way.

## What is this not settling?

- Whether readers ever sign in. Nothing on the front page needs an account, so it has no sign-in
  button; that question stays in the sharing design, item ⑨.
- Where the design pages live. They stay on the old address for now.
- When the old address stops serving the app. That waits until readers have moved.

## So what was decided?

**B**, by the owner on 11 November 2026: the app moves to hifth.app/read, the front page says
"Mac app — coming soon", and it collects emails for one message when the Mac app is out. The
work and what is still open are on [the site's design page](../design/hifth-app-site.md).
