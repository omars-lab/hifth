What should the highlighter look like, and what happens where two marks overlap?

Six shapes, four textures and seven rules for overlapping marks, all drawn on the same real page (page 42: the end of 2:254, the Throne Verse 2:255, the start of 2:256) at the width a phone gives the page. Where the difference only shows in motion — the mark being laid down, a second pass going over the first — it is live, with a button.

This page on the site: blog.bytesofpurpose.com/hifth/docs/design/highlight-texture-options-a.html

The short version
  1. Shape is the outline the pen leaves. Texture is what the ink does inside it. Overlap is what happens where a verse sits inside a passage, or a word run inside a verse.
  2. Each shape and texture is drawn twice: at reading size, then close up. Judge at reading size; the close-up only shows what you are looking at.
  3. Each overlap rule is drawn on two real overlaps: the verse inside a swept passage, and six words inside the verse.
  4. Under each: how well the letters read on the mark (4.5:1 is the reading floor), and how the mark stands against the paper (3:1 is the floor for a "you are here" sign).
  5. The recommendation is the last card: a rough band, a light grain, and a pass count that says how deep you are, never how many marks happen to cross.
What was settled before. Marks are drawn per line, never as one box around the run; nothing opaque goes over the letters; a reader may choose swipe, fill or outline and a strength in a few named steps; and the mark is blended into the print so the letters stay black. None of that is re-asked here. This page asks the next thing: the pen's own character, and what two marks do together.
