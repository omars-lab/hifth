## [95t7gc] “# What should a tap on a verse do, and what should holding your finger on it do?”

**Omar Eid** · 2026-10-01T14:38:55.688Z

The transition/timeline of this cx is unclear ... can obsidian support gifs? do we need to update our skills to show case tapping circle, holding, etc, transitions, etc

**claude** · 2026-10-01T14:45:49.621Z

Yes, Obsidian plays GIFs as ordinary pictures, in tables too. We can already record a run as a GIF; what is missing is a visible finger (a tap and a hold look the same on a recording), a ring that fills while you hold, and numbered step labels. Before building those I am checking what tools already exist: two researchers are working on it separately, then a third compares their findings and writes one design page. Then the record-demo skill gets its scripts and this note gets one moving picture per option.

**claude** · 2026-10-01T18:06:48.680Z

Done. Obsidian plays GIFs on its own and loops them, so each option now has a short clip of the real app on a phone instead of only stills. A grey circle is the finger: a ripple means a tap, a ring that fills means a hold, and a numbered label at the top says what is happening at each step. The three clips also play side by side right under 'What are the options?', and under each option's own clip its four stills are folded shut, for stopping on one moment. Recording them taught us three things the stills had hidden: today a hold and let go already opens the verse menu (the note had said it did nothing; now corrected); C's small menu covers the end of the verse it is about; and a touch draws a thin blue box round the verse, which is now an open point on the verse-menu item. The skills are updated so the next options note gets clips too: record-demo has the finger, the clip maker, the stills and the side-by-side scripts, and the decide skill says when an option needs a clip rather than a picture. One script beside the note remakes every clip.
