Where should the page tools sit on a phone?

On a computer, the tools for noting the page — bookmark, note, mistake, vowel-sign, word — sit in a bar of their own above the page. A phone has no mouse, no keyboard and little room, so that bar does not fit. Three ways of fitting it are built below, each in a real copy of the app. Hold each one and try a tool on the page; the owner chose C this way.

What should you try in each?

Pick the Word tool, then tap a word on the page: it opens into its parts. Pick Mistake and tap a word: it is marked. Then go back to plain reading. Notice how far your thumb went, whether the page's last line stayed in view, and whether you could tell which tool was on.

What does each one cost, and what does it lead to?

What it buysWhat it costsWhat it commits us to
A · A strip under the top bar
  • One tap switches tools, and every tool is always in sight.
  • Nothing to open or close, so nothing to learn.
  • It takes a row of the screen for good: on a phone the page's last lines drop below the fold.
  • It sits at the top, the hardest place for a thumb to reach one-handed.
The page shrinks by a row on every phone, whether or not the reader ever uses a tool.
B · A pen case in the bottom row
  • Costs no room: the page is as big as it is today.
  • The button's picture says which tool is on, and the fan names every tool.
  • Two taps to switch tools, every time.
  • The fan covers part of the page while it is open.
The one button has to carry the whole state of the tools; a reader who forgets a tool is on sees only its small picture.
C · A tray that slides up from the bottom row
  • Costs no room while closed, and sits where the thumb already is.
  • While open, switching is one tap, and the line under the tools says what a tap on the page will do.
  • Closing it puts the page back to plain reading, so a tool is never left on by accident.
  • While open it covers the bottom row: the page bar and the bookmarks are out of reach until it closes.
  • Two taps to the first tool.
Tool use becomes a mode the reader steps into and out of; the bottom row is where that mode lives.

Chosen: C, by the owner on 26 September 2026. It is the only one that keeps the whole page in view while reading and still leaves the tools by the thumb with a line saying what a tap will do; the bottom row it hides is not needed while noting.

What happens to A and B?

They stay here, still tryable, so the reason C was a choice can still be felt. The app shows C; the other two open only from this page.
