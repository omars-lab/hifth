What do we actually keep, and how do the tables join?

The store holds three tables. Two carry the page and the words; a third holds the source layout's own counts. Only one column in the whole store carries Qur'an text — the word's glyphs — and it lives here, in a store off the code and out of what the app ships, never in the repository and never in the build.

To draw a page, the app reads it straight off these two tables. Take the page's fifteen lines from the page table; for each line, pull the words whose id lands between its first_word_id and last_word_id from the word table. That is the entire join — one range lookup per line — and it holds because both tables run the same word id, 1 to 83,668, with no gaps. The check that every line's range resolves to real words, with nothing left dangling, passed when the copy was loaded on 8 September 2026.

Where did these two tables come from?

The outside library — the Quranic Universal Library — comes in eighteen files. We keep a copy of just two of them (the word text and the page plan, plus the two fonts that draw them) and measure our own work against the rest. Here is the whole library as one diagram: every file, and the exact key that clips one file to the next. Three backbones run through it — a word id down the middle, a verse key on the verse side, and a location triple the grammar files use.

What does each backbone do?

The word id — 1 to 83,668

The main spine. The page plan stores, for each printed line, its first and last word id; the word text carries the same ids on its own rows. So to draw a page you walk its lines and, for each line, pull the words whose id falls in that line's range. The two run the same 1-to-83,668 with no gaps — the single fact that makes drawing our own page possible at all. This is exactly the join the first picture draws between the two store tables.

The location — surah:ayah:word

The three grammar files — root, lemma, stem — point at a word by this triple. Not every word has all three (particles and some names have no triliteral root), so coverage is 60%, 87%, and 93% of the 83,668 words. What matters is the other direction: every grammar row points at a word that exists — no broken pointers — so the gap is only "some words have no root", never "a root points nowhere".

The verse key — surah:ayah — and the ordinal 1 to 6,236

Everything verse-level clips here: the per-verse grammar roll-ups, the themes, the topic lists, the two similar-verse sets, and the division ranges. The ayah registry is the hub — its ordinal and its verse key are what all of these join through.

Which of this do we hold, and which do we only measure against?

Held — two boxes, plus the two fonts. The word text and the page plan are the V4 pair the app keeps a copy of, so it can draw its own word-by-word page and stand it beside the printed one to check it. Both were loaded and verified on 8 September 2026. The two fonts that draw them are cleared to hold, but not loaded yet.
Reference only — everything else. The grammar files, the themes and topics, the two similar-verse sets, the division ranges: each is a ruler the app measures its own work against and links back to, keeping none of its bytes. Whether any of these is ever held is a separate, still-open question. The line between the two is the whole of the "copy or only measure?" decision, and it is why the word-text and font boxes carry a licence obligation the ruler boxes do not.
