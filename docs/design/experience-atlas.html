Hifth · the experience atlas

Where does this project's work actually live, and how do you get to it?

Hifth is one running app and a shelf of pages built to argue about how it should look and behave. Nothing before this page said where all of that lives in one place — a reader handed a single link had to already know that the app itself, the pages arguing over its details, and the pages that got published and forgotten were three different things kept in three different registers. This page is the front door to all three, plus the fourth: the way this project checks that any of the above is actually right.

The appWhat does Hifth actually do?

One line per thing a reader can do in the running app today, taken from the same map the codebase itself is oriented by — so this list cannot say more than the app actually does. There is no public link yet: today the app only runs from a helper on the machine that built it, and the check that would say whether a build is fit to hand to a stranger fails on purpose while the project stays private. That check exists and is run on demand; it is not silence, it is a known answer.

Ui language

Which language the chrome speaks — and the much longer list of things that never change with it.

Desktop spread

On a wide screen the app shows an open mus'haf — two leaves and a spine — and says plainly which half it does not have.

Offline pwa

Works with the phone in aeroplane mode, a week after you last opened it.

Word selection

Hold inside an ayah you have already selected, and the grain gets finer: the run under your finger is words, and dragging extends it.

Revision record

What the reader has opened, by day and by page, hizb or juz — the log, and the picture drawn from it. Never leaves the device.

Accessibility

Usable by someone who cannot see the page: labels on the polygons, a live region for what changed, and Arabic digits everywhere.

The instrumentHow does this project check that any of the above is right?

Every one of the pages above rests on a claim about the printed page — that a rectangle sits where it should, that a colour reads clearly, that a crop shows the right verse. Those claims get checked by a person sitting with the app and a real mus'haf open beside it, one verse at a time, and the settled answer for where that checking happens is described below rather than linked: the sessions themselves render real verses of the Qur'an, and this project ships none of that text to a page anyone outside it can open.

A checking session is opened by its address on the household's private network, from whichever phone or laptop is at hand. Every answer a person gives is posted back the instant it is given and appended to a plain record on that same machine — nothing is sent anywhere else, and nothing about a session is reachable from outside that network. The two published alternatives — handing a session to a stranger as a page, with or without a place for their answers to land — were drawn, compared, and set aside for the same reason: either one would put verses of the Qur'an on a server this project does not control, and nothing about how the rectangles line up is worth that trade.

Drawn from this project's own registers on every build: the app's shape from the map it is oriented by, the design calls and their pages from the decision register, and the homeless pages from the list of everything this project has published. Nothing on this page is typed a second time — if it ever disagrees with one of those, the register is right and this page has not been rebuilt. The decisions themselves, drawn in full with every option and every constraint between them, are one page over at the decision board linked above.
