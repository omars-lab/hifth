On a phone, how do you land on one exact page?

Four ways to drag the page bar, each one live below. Drag the round knob with your thumb on a phone, or with the mouse on a computer.

Still open. Nothing here is in the app yet; today's phone bar is option A. The one the owner picks is the one that gets built into the bar.
The page bar: the strip along the bottom of the app that you drag through all 604 pages, with a page-turn button at each end.  ·  The knob: the round handle you drag along it.  ·  Juz: one of the thirty roughly equal parts of the book; the bar is cut into thirty segments, one per juz.  ·  The magnifier: on a computer, the stretch of bar under the mouse spreads apart so single pages can be told apart. A phone has no mouse to hover with, so it never appears there.

What is being decided?

On a phone the bar is about 232 pixels across for 604 pages, so one pixel of thumb movement is about three pages. The question is what, if anything, should help a thumb stop on the page it wants.

Why ask now?

The computer got its magnifier this week, with single-page marks under the mouse. The phone, where a fat finger needs the help most, got nothing, because the magnifier works by hovering. The plan for the magnifier left the phone as its last step, to be tried by hand and chosen, not slipped in.

What happens if nobody decides?

Nothing breaks. The phone keeps option A: drag close, let go, then step with the page-turn buttons or type the page number. It works; it is just slow for a precise landing.

Try each one

Each bar gives you a page to aim for. Drag, let go, and it tells you how far off you landed. The dashed lines above the bar, shown while dragging, are where options B and D change speed.

What do other apps do?

  • Apple's own players (music, podcasts, video) slow the scrub as you slide your finger up: half, a quarter, then a tenth speed, and the knob rushes back under your finger as you return to the bar. Option B copies those speeds and heights exactly, from a detailed rebuild of the behaviour: UISlider with scrubbing speeds; also how it behaves for a user.
  • Video apps with chapters show a preview above the finger while scrubbing. That is the idea behind the strip in C.
  • Qur'an apps: we did not look again for this question. The look at the magnifier found list-and-jump menus rather than bars that help you land.

What have we already decided that this has to respect?

  • The bar's look: a thin track cut into thirty juz, with a round knob.
  • The magnifier on a computer, with page marks drawn only where they have room. The strip in C and D uses the same rule for which marks to draw.
  • A juz mark is a button, and the drag itself never snaps to a juz. Nothing here changes that.

What else was considered?

  • Press and hold to magnify the bar in place, like the computer's magnifier. Under a thumb you cannot see the marks you are spreading apart, so the strip (C) is the same idea moved to where you can see it.
  • A wheel of page numbers, like a date picker. It lands exactly, but it is a different control. The app already has a page-number box for typing.
  • Snap to every 5th page while dragging. It is precise to five, not to one, and it fights the rule that the drag itself never snaps.

What would change the answer?

Trying these on a real phone. Whether sliding up feels natural or awkward, and whether the strip helps or gets in the way, only show up under a real thumb. A mouse on this page is a fair first try, but not the test. If readers rarely want an exact page from the bar, and mostly step with the buttons once they are close, A is enough.

What is this not settling?

  • The computer: the magnifier stays as it is.
  • The exact speeds and heights. B and D use Apple's, and they can be tuned once one is chosen.
  • How a new reader learns about sliding up. That comes with the tutorial, if B or D wins.
