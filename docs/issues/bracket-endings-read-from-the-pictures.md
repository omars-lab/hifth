# A note ending on a bracket: the page text could not say whether the book prints a stop after it

**2026-10-08.** Many of the pitch's Study Quran notes end on a bracketed source or reference. The
book usually sets a full stop after the bracket, and the capture lost it. For most of them the
text read off the page pictures showed the stop, and those were put back. 29 notes were left
over: the page text either had no line matching the note's end, read it two different ways, or
seemed to show the bracket with nothing after it.

## What was tried first

Treat a "nothing after the bracket" reading as the book's own ending, and leave those notes
bare. And treat a note the page text could not find as sitting on a page that was never
photographed.

## What the evidence showed

Both were wrong. Every page in the commentary had been photographed; the page text had simply
garbled the note's last line, or the same words appeared in another note on another page and
the match landed there. And looking at the pictures of the "nothing after the bracket" notes,
most had the stop: the page text drops a stop at the end of a line, and misreads some initials
inside the bracket so the match fails. A rule that adds a stop after every closing bracket was
also ruled out: it would hide the notes the capture cut short, which end on a bracket too and
need their missing words, not a stop.

## What replaced it

Each of the 29 was found on its page by its verse, not by its last words, and the end of the note
was cropped and read by eye. 25 show the stop after the bracket and are on the hand-read list of
endings (a fingerprint of the note's last words and the page it was read on, never the words).
4 have no stop in the book itself and stay exactly as printed. None of the 29 turned out to be
cut short. The extractor's list of endings it cannot close now holds only those 4.

So the page text is a good way to find candidates and a poor judge of a small mark at the end of a
line; for a stop, the picture decides.
