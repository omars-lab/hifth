# Slanting a note's quotation of its own verse: right too rarely to use

**2026-10-08.** The book sets some words of its notes in italics: a note's quotation of the
verse it explains, a term being defined, a word given in its Arabic form, a book's title. The
pitch's notes show all of them upright, because neither the capture nor the text read off the
page pictures records the slant.

## What was tried

A shortcut that needs no slant at all: where a note repeats a run of its own verse's words, in
order, three or more long, slant that run. About 4,000 notes have at least one such run, so if
it held it would bring back most of the italics in the pitch.

## What the evidence showed

A sample of 25 runs, each found on its page picture by its verse and read by eye, was right
about three times in four. The misses fell into three kinds:

- the book quotes the verse in quotation marks and sets it upright;
- the run reaches past the slanted words, so one or two upright words would be slanted too;
- a plain phrase that happens to repeat the verse's words is not a quotation at all.

Some pictures could not be matched to the right line, which is a fault of the search, not of
the rule. And the other italics (terms, Arabic words, titles) cannot be found from the words.

## What replaced it

Nothing: the words stay upright. A panel that slants the wrong words a quarter of the time
reads worse than one that slants none, and tuning the rule against a sample risks fitting the
sample. Putting italics back needs a way to read the slant off the page pictures themselves,
which is listed in the commentary design page as what would answer it.
