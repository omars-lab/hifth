Omar, 2026-09-21: on an AskUserQuestion (the mark-placement sign-off fork), the options had
labels + a "cost" line + a preview, and he stopped me: **"pros/cons, implications, etc — this
should be a tenet on presenting options/decisions."**

**Why:** the person is choosing between *consequences*, not names. A label with a bare cost
hides the trade — what each option buys, what it risks, and what it commits you to next. He
wants to weigh the whole shape of each choice, side by side, before picking.

**How to apply:** for every option (in a quick in-chat AskUserQuestion as much as in a
checked-in decision record), give three things, not one:
- **Pros** — what it buys you.
- **Cons** — what it costs or risks.
- **Implications** — what it commits you to or rules out downstream: the next decision it
  forces, the door it closes, who it affects.
Lay them side by side as a rendered comparison (a small table, or each option's `preview`),
not a wall of prose; name the recommended one and why in one line. Now codified in the global
CLAUDE.md "When to stop and ask" section.
